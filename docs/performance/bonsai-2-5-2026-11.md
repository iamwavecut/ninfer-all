# Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, November 2026

The two NInfer artifacts of PrismML's
[Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B](https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Ternary-Bonsai-2.5-27B-GGUF) on an RTX PRO
6000, an RTX 5090, an RTX 4090 and an RTX 3090:
[PTQ1_0](https://huggingface.co/WaveCut/Ternary-Bonsai-2.5-27B-PTQ1_0-NInfer-v3) (6.17 GiB, the
release's 28-byte ternary blocks at 1.75 bits per weight) and
[PQ2_0](https://huggingface.co/WaveCut/Ternary-Bonsai-2.5-27B-PQ2_0-NInfer-v3) (7.37 GiB, the same
ternary values as `t2_g128_fp16` at 2.125 bits). MTP is PrismML's MTP head from the release's `-mtp`
GGUFs. Bonsai 2 and PrismML's own runtime ran on the same hosts as baselines. The setup is at the
[end](#how-these-were-measured).

- **One request without a drafter:** PTQ1_0 decodes fastest of the three ternary 27B artifacts on
  every card: 187.9 tok/s on the RTX PRO 6000 against PQ2_0's 167.2 and Bonsai 2's 167.1, 188.9 on
  the RTX 5090 (166.1, 166.5), 125.6 on the RTX 4090 (109.2, 109.2) and 94.1 on the RTX 3090 (92.8,
  92.8). This page's lane kernel added 5 to 7% to it on every card
  ([its activation](#the-lane-kernels-activation)).
- **MTP 3 and several requests:** PQ2_0 is the fastest on every card (MTP 3: 348.6, 344.9, 232.1
  and 189.4 tok/s on the RTX PRO 6000, 5090, 4090 and 3090), ahead of Bonsai 2 with ProCreations'
  head by 5 to 12% and of PTQ1_0 by 7 to 26%.
- **Prompts:** PQ2_0 reads a 261,120-token document in 73.4 s on the RTX PRO 6000 and 198.9 s on
  the RTX 3090; PTQ1_0 takes 13 to 17% longer.
- **Context:** with `rk4v4` both start at the engine's 1,048,576-token ceiling on the 32 and 96 GB
  cards; on the 24 GB cards PTQ1_0's 1.2 GiB smaller file leaves room for 61,440 to 65,536 more tokens.
- **PrismML's runtime:** NInfer decodes faster on every row of the RTX PRO 6000, 5090 and 4090, by
  8 to 11% without a drafter and 8 to 48% with MTP 3. On the RTX 3090 PrismML's runtime is faster
  with PTQ1_0: 95.3 against 94.1 tok/s without a drafter and 157.7 against 149.8 with MTP 3, where it
  accepts 0.58 of its drafts against NInfer's 0.49. It accepts more drafts on every card (0.54 to
  0.58 against 0.47 to 0.51).
- **Quality:** the two artifacts' perplexities differ by 0.03% on the quick corpus; Bonsai 2.5 reads
  plain text with a higher perplexity than Bonsai 2 (6.667 against 5.631).

## The PTQ1_0 kernels

A PTQ1_0 block holds 128 ternary values in 28 bytes: 24 bytes of five base-3 digits, 2 bytes of
four, and a binary16 scale ([GGUF block formats](../gguf.md#formats)). A byte's digits are values 16
apart (8 apart in the last eight of the 24 bytes), so the eight 4-value words of one 32-value slice
come from different bytes and digits. NInfer multiplies the blocks as stored:

| Columns | Kernel | Runs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (up to 8 before sm_80) | the lane kernel: a lane takes whole blocks and walks each byte's digits in value order, four bytes at a time; two rows share each activation load; 8 lanes take a pair of rows, 16 or 32 when the product's row pairs would fill fewer than six warps an SM or a lane would walk more than 12 blocks; the activation is transposed ([below](#the-lane-kernels-activation)) | decode without a drafter, and drafting through the output or proposal head |

| 21 and more | llama.cpp's `mul_mat_q` with a tile loader that expands each block to int8 -1, 0 and 1, staged through `cp.async` where the card's profile measured a gain ([what is calibrated](../device-profiles.md#what-is-calibrated)) | prompt chunks |

- **Bits.** Every slice's dot is an exact integer in both vector kernels, and a column's sum does
  not depend on the other columns. The lane kernel and the tensor-core kernel add a row's blocks in
  different orders, so a verification call's sums can differ from a decode call's in their last
  bits, and an answer decoded with MTP can differ from the same answer without a drafter at a
  near-tie. One-column kernels in the tensor-core kernel's order, which would keep the bits equal,
  were 1.3 times slower than that build's lane kernel on the down product (RTX 5090: 19.7 and 20.2
  µs against 14.9).
- **The 20-column limit** is one constant for every card. In a build that let the tensor-core
  kernel take up to 32 columns, the matrix kernel was faster from 24 columns on the RTX 4090 and
  5090 (the layer: 251.9 against 263.3 µs and 200.7 against 217.1); on the RTX 3090 the
  tensor-core kernel would also win from 21 to 32 columns (357.5 against 442.3 µs at 24, 433.2
  against 448.6 at 32), which six to eight requests with MTP 3 reach.
- **PQ2_0 against PTQ1_0.** PTQ1_0 reads 18% fewer weight bytes but spends more integer
  instructions on each value. It is the faster one at one column on every card (RTX 3090: 94.1
  against 92.8 tok/s), and the slower one wherever more columns share each weight read: MTP
  verification, several requests, and prompts, whose tile loader expands five digits a byte.
- **Rejected.** Fewer K ranges a group in the tensor-core kernel, so that a CTA holds two or four
  16-row groups: the fastest count differs by product and card (the 6,144-row input product 6 to
  30% faster at four ranges on the RTX 4090, 5090 and PRO 6000; the RTX 4090's down product 13%
  slower), and the best count for each card gains 1.7 to 3.7% of the layer's products at 2 to 20
  columns. For the lane kernel: a separate plane of each slice's int32 sum beside the untransposed
  activation (short chat 1.1% faster on the RTX 5090, 1.5% slower on the RTX 3090, whose down
  product took 8% longer); each slice loading its own scale and sum before Blackwell (RTX 3090 1.6%
  slower, RTX 4090 unchanged); and more warps an SM before a row pair takes more lanes (8 or 12
  instead of 6 changes only the RTX 3090's down product, which the 12-block cap covers; 16 makes the
  6,144-row input product 2 µs slower on the RTX 5090).

### The lane kernel's activation

ggml's `q8_1` activation keeps each column's int8 values in order, then one (scale, sum) pair per 32
values. The lane kernel reads a transposed form, in the order of PrismML's kernel: value `e` of the
128-value block `b` lies at byte (`e` / 16) · (K / 128) · 16 + 16 `b` + `e` mod 16 of its column, so
the 8 to 32 lanes of a row pair, which take neighbouring blocks, read neighbouring 16 bytes; and each
pair's sum is the exact integer total of its 32 int8 values, which the slice's dot subtracts as it
is. On Blackwell (sm_120) each slice loads its own scale and sum (4 bytes); the older cards load a
block's four pairs at once (16 bytes). The tensor-core and matrix kernels keep the untransposed
form. A slice's dot is the same exact integer either way: the greedy answer of the lane kernel on
the untransposed and on the transposed activation came out byte for byte equal on every card.

**Hardware.** Rented single-GPU hosts on Vast.ai, each card at its default power limit, 24 and
25 November 2026:

| Card | Power limit | Host CPU | PCIe | Driver |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Workstation Edition | 600 W | AMD Ryzen 7 9700X, 16 threads | 5.0 x16 | 580.178.04 |
| RTX 5090 | 575 W | AMD Ryzen Threadripper 3990X, 128 threads | 4.0 x16 | 580.173.02 |
| RTX 4090 | 450 W | AMD EPYC 7742, 30 of its 256 threads | 4.0 x16 | 595.99.02 |
| RTX 3090 | 420 W | AMD EPYC 7532, 64 threads | 4.0 x16 | 595.84 |

The server runs pinned to the CPUs of the GPU's NUMA node. The [older cards](#older-cards) ran on
Vast.ai hosts as well, each at its default power limit: the Tesla V100-FHHL-16GB with driver
580.196.07, the Quadro RTX 6000 with 615.71.10 and the A100-SXM4-40GB with 610.57.04.

**Software.** NInfer from this page's branch, built with CUDA 13.4.3 for `86;89;120a`, each card on
its built-in device profile; the build before this page's kernels is `f84786d99f`. The older cards
ran the same source built for `75;80;86;89;120a` with CUDA 13.4.3 and, on the Tesla V100, for
`70;75;80;86;89` with CUDA 12.9.2. PrismML's runtime as [above](#prismmls-runtime).

**Server.** `ninfer-serve <artifact> --max-context 262144 --kv-capacity 262144 --max-concurrency 1
--kv-dtype rk8v4 --gdn-state-fp16`, plus `--spec mtp --draft-tokens 3` for MTP. Everything else is
at its default; every request starts with a fresh nonce, so prefix reuse never hits.

**Requests** ([`tools/bench/refbench.py`](../../tools/bench/refbench.py)), greedy, thinking off,
non-streaming, against the OpenAI Chat Completions endpoint: *short chat* is the mean decode rate of
five 512-token answers (an essay, a Python module with tests, a word problem, a Russian
explanation, a list of forty items); a *depth* is a cold document of that many tokens (PG-19,
WikiText and this repository's text) followed by the request to retell its final part in about 400
words; *needles* plant three codes at 33, 66 and 90% of a document 1,024 tokens short of the
window and ask them back in order. Prompt time is the server's time to the first token; VRAM is
the device memory the idle server holds (NInfer plans the KV cache, workspaces and graphs at
startup).

**Kernel timings** ([`ninfer_gguf_linear_bench`](../../bench/ops/gguf_linear_bench.cu)): the public
Linear and LinearSwiGLU Ops at the 27B's shapes with random PTQ1_0 blocks, the activation's int8
quantization inside the timing, the median of 30 samples, each after a 256 MiB read that evicts L2.
